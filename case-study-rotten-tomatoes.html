Redesign: Rotten Tomatoes

Designing a native mobile app for Rotten Tomatoes — fixing 7 distinct usability pain points through participatory research, a gallery-first layout, and an in-app box office.

UX/UI Designer·Sketch · Marvel3 weeks

🎯 Problem

Rotten Tomatoes had no official mobile app — only a non-responsive website littered with inconsistent navigation, text-heavy layouts, and third-party ticket redirects that kicked users out mid-task. Participatory observation with movie enthusiasts surfaced 7 distinct pain points: text-heavy, non-consistent nav, inappropriate bottom nav on mobile web, unnecessary items, no hierarchy, inconsistent grids, and frustrating external redirects.

💡 Solution

A native mobile app with gallery-first browsing, simplified navigation (5→4 items), reorganized movie detail hierarchy, and an in-app box office — eliminating the Fandango redirect that was users' top frustration.

🔀 Flows

[ Gallery-first movie feed + ratings ]
DiscoveryPoster-first card grid with Tomatometer score visible at a glance — matches how users actually identify movies.
[ In-app box office + showtimes ]
TicketingNative box office eliminates the Fandango redirect — users select movie, showtime, and purchase without leaving the app.

🧠 Key Decisions

  • Gallery over list layout. Research showed users identify movies by poster, not by name. A card-first gallery reduced cognitive load and matched how audiences actually browse.
  • Navigation simplified from 5 to 4 items. Removed "TV" and "More" as separate tabs, folding TV into the main feed and log-in into a profile tab.
  • In-app ticketing instead of external redirects. Designed a native box office — solved the #1 user frustration without requiring a third-party partnership change.

📊 Impact

Results
  • 7 usability pain points identified through participatory observation research
  • Complete hi-fi mobile app redesign delivered in 3 weeks
  • Navigation simplified from 5 to 4 items — reduced cognitive load
  • In-app box office designed to eliminate disruptive third-party redirects

📖 Learning

The best redesigns start with deep respect for what already exists — understanding why something was built the way it was makes you a sharper, more empathetic critic of it.
