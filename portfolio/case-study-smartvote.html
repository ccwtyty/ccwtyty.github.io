SmartVote

Design a system to educate voters about the election, candidates, and policies — so they make well-informed decisions before casting a virtual ballot.

UX/UI Designer·Figma1 Semester · Fall 2020

🎯 Problem

Online voting has no dedicated UX — the process is long, complex, and the forms are confusing. More critically, not all voters do adequate research before casting a vote. In 2020, COVID-19 added urgency: how could Americans vote safely from home without sacrificing the integrity of an informed decision?

💡 Solution

SmartVote requires voters to complete educational courses about candidates and policies before accessing the ballot — using quiz-like prompts to ensure knowledge gain, not just passive reading. Voters track their progress and can favorite candidates with a star badge throughout the process.

🔀 Flows

[ Course modules — learn before you vote ]
Education FlowComplete courses to unlock ballot access — quiz-like prompts ensure active engagement, not just scrolling.
[ Virtual ballot with candidate info + progress ]
Voting FlowTrack voting progress and learn more about candidates inline — favorited candidates shown with star badge.

🧠 Key Decisions

  • Mandatory course completion before voting. The most deliberate design choice — requiring voters to engage with education before the ballot ensures informed decisions, even at the cost of added friction. Research validated: 5/5 users said they wanted this.
  • Quiz-as-learning, not quiz-as-gate. Courses feel exploratory rather than pass/fail — reducing anxiety while still driving meaningful engagement with candidate information.
  • Progress tracking throughout. Borrowed patterns from gaming and e-learning to keep users motivated and aware of how far they are in the process.

📊 Impact

Results
  • 5/5 research participants said they'd benefit from an app requiring educational modules before voting
  • 5/5 agreed that voters often make uninformed decisions — validating the core premise
  • Post-prototype feedback unanimously positive on progress tracking, stance summaries, and beginner-friendly onboarding

📖 Learning

Designing for a politically sensitive topic requires leading with clarity and neutrality — the design must never appear to favor a viewpoint, only to inform one.
