Atlas: Weather + GPS All-in-One

Help drivers plan better and drive safely by combining hyper-accurate weather prediction and navigation in a single app — so you know it's going to snow before you're already on the freeway.

UX/UI Designer · Figma 1 Semester · Fall 2020

🎯 Problem

Drivers are often caught in bad weather because checking weather and navigating require two separate apps. Weather apps don't show conditions en route, and navigation apps don't surface weather until it's already a problem. Sudden weather changes — fog, ice, flash storms — give drivers no time to reroute or stop.

💡 Solution

A single app combining turn-by-turn navigation with hyper-local weather prediction en route. Drivers see weather conditions at every leg of their journey before leaving, get real-time alerts when conditions change mid-drive, and can reschedule departure if bad weather is predicted.

🧠 Key Decisions

  • Combined weather and navigation in a single app. Based on the insight that drivers checking these separately creates a complex, time-consuming process — and a gap between the last weather check and actual conditions.
  • Real-time alerts over static forecasts. Sudden weather changes are the problem drivers can't plan for — the design centered on in-drive alerting, not just pre-trip forecasting.
  • Departure scheduling as a safety feature. Suggested alternative departure times when bad weather was predicted — giving drivers agency to avoid conditions entirely rather than react to them.

🤝 Collaboration

Developed in Project Design & Implementation course at RIT. Conducted user research using driver personas grounded in real commuter behavior — findings directly informed flow decisions.

📊 Impact

Results
  • Identified 3 core pain points through user research
  • Designed full navigation + weather experience from research to hi-fi — route planning and real-time alert flows
  • Demonstrated ability to design for safety-critical use cases where cognitive load constraints are non-negotiable

📖 Learning

Safety-critical design demands radical clarity — every word and element on screen competes with the road, so restraint is the most important design choice you can make.
